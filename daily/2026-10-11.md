## Top papers

- **When Predicting Nothing Beats SAM 3: Revisiting Evaluation in Video Object Segmentation** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02946). *(Early October.)* Going by the title, a trivial baseline that predicts no mask scores better than SAM 3 under some current VOS evaluation setting. The likely cause is how frames where the object is absent or occluded are scored. If you choose video segmenters by J&F on public benchmarks, read this before trusting the leaderboard.
- **GRAFT: Growing Agglomerative Foundation Models via Continual Teacher Distillation** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02597). *(Early October.)* Agglomerative models (AM-RADIO-style) distill several teachers, such as DINO, SigLIP and SAM, into one student backbone. GRAFT appears to add teachers one at a time instead of all at once. It is worth checking whether this lets you extend a deployed backbone with a new teacher without full retraining.
- **Referring Multi-Object Tracking in Moving-Camera Videos via Global Motion Compensation** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04185). *(Early October.)* Referring MOT tracks the objects that match a text query. This paper compensates for camera ego-motion, which matters for drone, dashcam and handheld footage, where motion models built for static cameras break down.
- **Lightweight and Resource-Efficient Perception for Robotic Guide Dogs** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03187). *(Early October.)* An embedded perception stack for an assistive legged robot. Read it as a case study in fitting detection and segmentation into a tight compute budget, not as a new method.

## Today's deep read

**When Predicting Nothing Beats SAM 3** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02946).

- **What to read:** which benchmarks and metric variants the empty prediction wins on, how frames where the target is absent are scored, and whatever corrected protocol the authors propose. Also check whether the ranking of SAM 2, SAM 3 and other trackers changes under that protocol.
- **How it could apply to you:** most production video segmentation errors happen on disappearance and reappearance: false masks on absent objects, and lost re-identification after occlusion. If your internal evaluation uses the same metric code as the public benchmarks, it may be rewarding or hiding exactly those errors. Report absent-frame false positives and re-acquisition latency as separate numbers alongside J&F.
