## Top papers

- **ChangeJEPA: Predict Before You Compare for Remote Sensing Change Detection** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05570), [project](https://buddhi19.github.io/ChangeJEPA). Wijenayake, Jayasundara, Patel. Rather than subtracting features from two co-registered images, the model first predicts the second image's representation from the first, JEPA-style, and then compares the prediction with what was actually observed. It reports gains of up to 1.51 IoU points over a decoder-matched Siamese baseline on SYSU-CD, LEVIR-CD+ and CDD. **Why it matters:** the matched-decoder comparison isolates the effect of the pretext. The same idea applies to any "before/after" inspection task (infrastructure, retail shelves, industrial defects), not just satellites. Abstract read from the listing only.
- **When Does Sharing Work? Scaling a Shared SigLIP2 Across Image, Video, Audio, and Audio-Visual Representations (Omni-Encoder)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05406). Sun, Wang, Liu et al. One SigLIP2 encoder with a single shared parameter set, trained jointly on image, video, audio and audio-visual data. The abstract reports "competitive results on 16 benchmarks" and says that joint training improves each modality over its separately trained counterpart, but gives no numbers. Code and models are promised. **Why it matters:** if the image numbers hold up, a single encoder for frames and audio could simplify multimodal surveillance or video-QA stacks. Wait for the tables and weights before you swap your SigLIP2 checkpoint.

## Models & releases

- **Ultralytics 8.4.203** — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ultralytics/8.4.203/). Uploaded Nov 5, 19:13 UTC. From the 8.4.202→8.4.203 sdist diff:
  - **New `format=nxp` export** compiles an INT8 LiteRT model for the i.MX95 Neutron NPU with `eiq-neutron-sdk==3.3.0`. Export runs only on Linux x86_64. At inference time on the board, `AutoBackend` loads `*_nxp.tflite` through `tflite_runtime` and `libneutron_delegate.so`. INT8 is enforced, calibration data defaults per task, and the end-to-end (NMS-free) head is disabled for this format.
  - **Loading hardening:** dataset `.cache` files are now read with a restricted unpickler that accepts only NumPy arrays and paths. SAM, SAM 3, YOLO-NAS and text-embedding caches load with `weights_only`. A checkpoint that needs a missing module now raises `ModuleNotFoundError` instead of auto-installing it, which may break old custom checkpoints.
  - **Fixes:**
    - `hand-keypoints.yaml` `flip_idx` is now the identity mapping. Flipping one hand doesn't swap its fingers, so the old mapping corrupted labels under `fliplr`.
    - The SciPy tracker-matching fallback now clips costs at the threshold, as `lap` does.
    - FastSAM box prompts that hit no mask no longer select a spurious mask.
    - `backend.fp16` now follows `.to(dtype)`.
    - ExecuTorch export always runs on CPU.
- **supervision 0.31.5** — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/supervision/0.31.5/), [changelog](https://github.com/roboflow/supervision/blob/develop/docs/changelog.md).
  - `LineZone` keeps an object's side for `max(30, threshold+1)` absent frames, so crossings during occlusion now count. Pass `max_absent_frames` to restore the old behaviour.
  - COCO export computes areas with Python scalars, so a 300×300 float16 box exports 90000 rather than infinity. It also keeps polygon holes.
  - OBB IoU/NMS now uses an exact clipper: correct, but about 10x slower per pair, so crowded rotated-box scenes may take seconds.
  - Also: RTL (Arabic/Hebrew) labels, grayscale heatmaps, and a fix to the GHUM wrist edge.
- **ExecuTorch 1.6.0 / cuDNN 9.28.0:** still no notes. GitHub still marks v1.5.1 as latest and the cuDNN notes page was refused. (Both were first covered Nov 5.)
- **Release check (PyPI):** unchanged for torch 2.15.0, torchvision 0.29.2, transformers 5.22.1, timm 1.0.31, rfdetr 1.11.2, Roboflow Inference 1.8.4, SAHI 0.14.0, ONNX Runtime 1.31.1, ONNX 1.23.2, TensorRT 11.4.0.106, OpenVINO 2026.4.1, LiteRT 2.3.0, Triton 3.9.0, torchao 0.18.0, flash-attn 2.8.3.post2, Kornia 0.9.0 and Model Optimizer 0.48.0. ## Edge & deployment

- **NXP i.MX95 joins the Ultralytics NPU list** (after Hailo, Ascend, Xilinx, Axelera, DeepX, RKNN, IMX and QNN). The flow is `yolo export format=nxp model=yolo26n.pt data=...`: Ultralytics exports a LiteRT INT8 model and then the Neutron converter compiles it. The model runs on the NPU only through the vendor delegate, so CPU fallback for unsupported ops is something to profile on real hardware. No latency numbers are published yet.

## Today's deep read

**Ultralytics 8.4.203: NXP export and loading hardening** — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ultralytics/8.4.203/) (diff `ultralytics/engine/exporter.py`, `nn/backends/litert.py`, `data/utils.py`, `nn/tasks.py`).

- **What to read:**
  - `export_nxp` and the `isolated-nxp` export environment (Python 3.13, NXP's extra package index).
  - The restricted `Unpickler` in `load_dataset_cache_file`.
  - The removed auto-install branch in `torch_safe_load`.
- **Why it matters:** two classic pickle code-execution paths are now closed: a poisoned `labels.cache` shipped with a dataset, and a checkpoint that pulls in a pip package. It also adds a mid-range NPU SoC that is common in industrial and automotive boards.
- **How it could apply to you:**
  - **Upgrading:** load each of your production checkpoints once in CI. If any were saved with custom modules, they now fail loudly instead of installing packages. Fix that by re-saving the weights from a clean environment.
  - **Hand pose:** if you fine-tuned on `hand-keypoints.yaml` with `fliplr>0`, half your flipped samples had finger chains swapped. Retrain and compare per-keypoint OKS (keypoint accuracy).
  - **Evaluating i.MX95:** export a small YOLO26 at INT8 with representative calibration data, then check accuracy against your ONNX FP32 baseline before you profile latency on the board.
