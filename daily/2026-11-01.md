## Edge & deployment

- **Per-channel ONNX INT8 in Ultralytics 8.4.196** — [release](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/releases/tag/v8.4.196), PR #26978. For opset 13 and newer, INT8 weights now get one scale per output channel instead of one for the whole tensor; older opsets keep per-tensor. Reported COCO results: detection mAP 0.3557 → 0.3814, segmentation box mAP 0.3213 → 0.3595, mask mAP 0.2862 → 0.3046. The notes don't name the model size, give no latency numbers, and warn that some classes may still score lower. All 167 ONNX tests passed, and 51 INT8 artifacts ran on ONNX Runtime 1.20.0.
- **YOLO26 on DGX Spark: use TensorRT FP16, not INT8, at batch 1** (#26968, docs). Ultralytics found INT8 slower for the smaller models and less accurate for every model tested. That fits a wider pattern: at batch 1, small detectors are often limited by kernel launches and memory traffic, not by compute, so INT8 adds quantize/dequantize overhead without much to gain.

## Today's deep read

**Ultralytics #26978: per-channel ONNX INT8 weight quantization** — [release](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/releases/tag/v8.4.196).

- **What to read:** the PR diff linked from the release. Check three things: which quantizer it calls (ONNX Runtime `quantize_static` with `per_channel=True` or something custom), how activations are calibrated (data, number of images, method), and why it falls back to per-tensor below opset 13 (per-axis `QuantizeLinear`/`DequantizeLinear` only exist from opset 13).
- **Why it matters:** conv weights can vary in range by orders of magnitude from one output channel to the next, more so after BN folding. With one scale for the whole tensor, channels with small weights get rounded to a few levels or to zero. A gain of 2.6 mAP points for detection and 3.8 box-mAP points for segmentation, just from changing how scales are assigned, suggests that previous per-tensor ONNX INT8 exports were losing a lot of accuracy.
- **How it could apply to you:**
  - **If you ship Ultralytics ONNX INT8 models,** re-export with 8.4.196+ and evaluate them on your own validation set, per class, since the notes warn some classes may drop. Measure latency on your target too: per-channel scales are cheap on ONNX Runtime CPU and most NPUs, but some runtimes handle per-axis Q/DQ more slowly.
  - **If you quantize through your own pipeline** (ORT, TensorRT, OpenVINO NNCF, ExecuTorch), check that weights are per-channel. TensorRT and NNCF use per-channel weights by default; ORT `quantize_static` does not (`per_channel=False`).
  - **Watch the new re-export behavior** (#26941): the output directory is now cleaned on re-export, so don't keep calibration caches or hand-edited files there.
