# CV Digest — Wednesday, October 7, 2026

## Top papers

- **Kandinsky 6.0 Video: Foundation Models for Synchronized Video and Audio Generation** — [link](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05608). A dual-stream CrossDiT joins a pretrained video stream to a newly trained audio stream via bidirectional cross-attention, producing 5 s Full-HD clips with synced 44 kHz audio; RL post-training cuts speech errors by ~47%. Notable because both the 3B Lite and 29B Pro checkpoints ship under MIT.
- **Rethinking Long-Video Efficiency: A Joint Allocation Perspective on Frames, Pixels, and Front-End Latency** — [link](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04318). For long-video VLMs, many low-res frames beat few native-res frames at the same token budget, and on hour-long inputs *video decoding* — not the model — dominates wall time. Their training-free LoHi scheme (dense low-res stream + a few high-res frames picked from codec metadata or query relevance) gains ~10.6 pts while cutting decode latency up to 7×.
- **In-Distribution Forcing for Long Video Generation at Test Time** — [link](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03120). Argues long-video drift comes from cached KV entries themselves going out-of-distribution; "self-caching" each chunk independently keeps the cache aligned with training conditions, stretching short-horizon video diffusion models to minute-scale clips with less color/texture/motion decay — no retraining needed.
- **HLA-WM: Hybrid Linear Attention for Long-Horizon Video World Models** — [link](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05739). Mixes softmax attention with recurrent linear attention plus geometry-guided retrieval of past states, so a world model can recall earlier scene content without a full KV cache: ~12× less memory, +0.74 dB PSNR and 28.5% lower rotation error on 60 s rollouts at ≤1.6% throughput cost.

## Models & releases

- **Kandinsky 6.0 Video Lite (3B) / Pro (29B)** — [link](https://huggingface.co/kandinskylab/Kandinsky-6.0-Lite-5s-Diffusers). Open text-to-video+audio weights in Diffusers format under MIT, with CPU offload and tiled super-resolution for memory-limited GPUs; code at [kandinskylab/kandinsky-6](https://github.com/kandinskylab/kandinsky-6). The Lite model is the realistic one to try on a single workstation GPU.

## GPU / CUDA / performance

- **Control How Your GPU Shares Work with Green Contexts** — [link](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/control-how-your-gpu-shares-work-with-green-contexts/). Green contexts let one process carve out SM and workqueue partitions and pin streams to them; CUDA 13.1 brings them to the Runtime API (`cudaGreenCtxCreate`, `cudaExecutionCtxStreamCreate`). On a 148-SM Blackwell GPU, a latency-critical kernel next to bulk work drops from 0.14 ms (high-priority stream) to 0.007 ms with a dedicated partition.
- **Evolution of the PyTorch Media Processing Landscape** — [link](https://pytorch.org/blog/evolution-of-the-pytorch-media-processing-landscape/). TorchCodec becomes the single place for image/video/audio decode and encode (CPU and CUDA); `torchvision.io.read_video` and `VideoReader` are deprecated and torchvision narrows to transforms. If your data loaders still decode video through torchvision, plan the migration now.
- **Modernizing Table Batched Embeddings with FBTriton** — [link](https://pytorch.org/blog/modernizing-table-batched-embeddings-with-fbtriton/). Meta rewrote its Table Batched Embedding kernels from CUDA to Triton and got a median 1.28× forward speedup with simpler, portable code (Hopper, Blackwell, AMD). Recsys-focused, but a useful data point on Triton replacing hand-written CUDA in production.
