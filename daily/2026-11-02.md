## TL;DR
- **ReLift turns 2D tracks plus depth into dense 3D tracks, then repairs them with a small feed-forward corrector.** On WorldTrack it beats the strongest baselines by 2.1 APD on all points and 3.0 APD on dynamic points, and runs 2.7–4.4x faster than 4RC. The corrector sees only geometry, so the same model works with any 2D tracker or depth model.

## Top papers

Monday's cs.CV listing has 102 papers; we could read the titles of the first 50. None of those is about 2D detection or a new backbone, so today's picks lean toward 3D.

- **ReLift: Dense 3D Tracking as Lifted Trajectory Repair** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42136), [project](https://relift3d.github.io/), [code](https://github.com/ReLift3D/ReLift). Singer, Khriesh, Rotstein, Kasten, Litany. The authors start from a simple baseline: track points in 2D with a pretrained tracker, lift them to 3D with estimated depth and cameras, and interpolate through occlusions. That baseline already rivals dedicated 3D trackers. Its two failure modes are inherited correspondence and depth errors, and occluded points that take the occluder's depth. ReLift adds a feed-forward corrector that alternates attention along each trajectory and across trajectories, and predicts 3D residuals. It is trained only on 3D trajectories with simulated lifting errors, never on images. Results on WorldTrack: state-of-the-art mean accuracy, +2.1 APD (all points) and +3.0 APD (dynamic points) over the strongest baselines, and 2.7–4.4x faster than 4RC. The project page gives no absolute APD values and no runtime hardware.
- **PGGT-Loc: Primitive-Grounded Geometry Transformer for Feed-Forward Camera Localization** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41833), [code](https://github.com/3dv-casia/PGGT-Loc). NeurIPS 2026. This method localizes a single query image against planar map primitives, such as floor plans, layout models or dense surface meshes, instead of SfM point clouds or image databases that need upkeep. One forward pass predicts several localization targets, and the model works in unseen environments without per-scene training. The authors report state-of-the-art results on both dense surface maps and minimal layout models. The abstract gives no numbers. It is worth a look if you build indoor robots or AR apps and already have CAD or BIM maps.
- **Rethinking Asymmetry in Self-Supervised 3D Geometry Learning** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42092), [project](https://gynjn.github.io/rethinking-asymmetry/). Kang, Cheng, Ye, Lyu, Yang, Park. This paper trains 3D geometry (depth and pose) from unposed images with no labels. Methods of this kind hold some views out and must predict them. The authors argue that holding views out of the *input* is the wrong place to do it. Instead, a shared backbone sees every view and uses all of them to find correspondences, and the held-out views are left out only when the scene representation is built. Sparse-view features are also aligned to dense-view features and to a frozen vision foundation model, which adds more training signal without labels. The abstract reports better depth and pose than prior self-supervised methods; we could not read the numbers.
- **A Patch is More Than a Token (PMTT)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41814), [code](https://github.com/zixiongwang-ai/PMTT) (MIT). Standard ViT features give one vector per patch. PMTT instead describes each patch by a set of identity-preserving views of it ("Dense Orbital Views"). The *Observed* variant keeps the canonical state plus 16 views. The *Manifold* variant compresses them into a local geometry of rank ≤ 8. The backbone is frozen DINOv3 (S, B, L, H+). Evaluations cover SPair-71k, DAVIS-2017, MegaDepth-1500, ScanNet-1500, NYUv2 and ADE20K. arXiv blocked the abstract and the repo page shows no numbers (they are in its RESULTS.md), so we can't say yet how much it gains over plain DINOv3 patch tokens, or at what cost.

## Edge & deployment

- **NVIDIA Model Optimizer 0.48.0** — [changelog](https://nvidia.github.io/Model-Optimizer/reference/0_changelog.html), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/nvidia-modelopt/). Uploaded Nov 2 (11:05 UTC), after three release candidates. Most of it is for LLMs. The parts that matter for vision:
  - **New recipes:** INT8 and FP8 recipes for timm ResNets that also quantize the inputs to residual adds. Without that, TensorRT can't fuse the add into the INT8 conv and falls back to higher precision around each block. ResNet supports only INT8 and FP8, without AutoQuantize. There is also a full BEVFormer ONNX PTQ example (INT8 and FP8, TensorRT engine build, nuScenes evaluation).
  - **ONNX fixes:** INT8 entropy calibration now works for models with FP16 activations. Quantized MaxViT-style attention with transposed weights now exports. Scale and zero-point tensors no longer reuse names. A DLA scalar-operand bug is fixed, and FP16 output types are kept.
  - **TensorRT 11:** fixes to ONNX AutoTune benchmarking, runtime initialization, custom plugin builds, and preprocessing with plugin libraries.
  - **Breaking:** transformers ≥ 5.5 is required (4.x dropped), and `ort_patching` and `graph_utils` are removed, with a migration map to the new modules. The single-format CLI flags (`--qformat` and others) are deprecated in favor of `--recipe`. Diffusers ONNX export and TensorRT-LLM checkpoint export are deprecated, with removal planned for 0.49.0 at the earliest.

## Today's deep read

**ReLift: Dense 3D Tracking as Lifted Trajectory Repair** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42136), [project](https://relift3d.github.io/), [code](https://github.com/ReLift3D/ReLift).

- **What to read:** Section 1 for the claim that the plain baseline (lift 2D tracks with depth, interpolate through occlusions) already rivals dedicated 3D trackers. Then look at how the lifting errors are simulated for training: what kinds of depth noise, correspondence drift and occluder-depth errors they inject. Training on simulated errors only works if those errors look like the real ones. Finally, the speed/accuracy table: which settings are the "faster configurations", and how much accuracy they give up.
- **Why it matters:** the design is modular. You keep whatever 2D tracker and depth or camera model you already run, and add a small corrector that never sees pixels. When a better depth model comes out, you swap it in without retraining the corrector, and the authors say a single model works across trackers and geometry models.
- **How it could apply to you:**
  - **If you track in 3D from a single camera** (robot manipulation, sports analytics, AR occlusion), try the baseline first: your 2D tracker plus a geometry model such as VGGT. Then measure what the corrector adds, especially on dynamic points, where the paper's gain is largest (+3.0 APD).
  - **The occlusion failure mode applies to any 2D-to-3D lifting.** When a point is hidden, its depth sample comes from whatever is in front of it. If you lift 2D detections or keypoints with a depth map, you have the same bug. At minimum, mark samples taken during occlusion and don't trust their depth.
  - **Check the runtime yourself:** "2.7–4.4x faster than 4RC" is relative, and the page names no GPU. Time the full pipeline (2D tracker + depth + corrector) on your own hardware, since the 2D tracker and depth model will likely cost more than the corrector.
