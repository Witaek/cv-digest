## TL;DR
- **VPM (KAIST, ETH, NYU, SNU) is a multi-view video world model that also forecasts 3D point trajectories.** It is built on NVIDIA Cosmos3-Nano. Code, a 10K-clip simulated dataset and a 1,200-clip benchmark are out now. The abstract claims point supervision improves both video fidelity and 3D motion accuracy over a video-only baseline, but it gives no numbers.
- **MaSS (Tongji, NeurIPS 2026) is self-supervised monocular depth that gets better with mixed-domain training.** One model trained on KITTI, DDAD, NYUv2 and nuScenes together beats the per-dataset models on all four (KITTI Abs Rel 0.092) and on all eight zero-shot sets. It uses a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14, and the weights are on HF.
- **supervision 0.31.1 fixes metric bugs.** MeanAverageRecall now skips COCO crowd/ignored targets and matches in confidence order, and float16 box areas, mAP and `with_nmm` no longer overflow. If you report mAR or mAP from supervision 0.31.0, re-run.

## Top papers

- **Learning the 4D World through Point Trajectory Forecasting (VPM)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28594), [code](https://github.com/rootyJeon/VPM), [project](https://rootyjeon.github.io/video-point-model/), [VPM-Data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rooty2020/VPM-Data), [VP-WorldBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rooty2020/VP-WorldBench). From Jeon, Hong et al., with Pollefeys, Joo, Xie and Shin (KAIST, ETH Zürich, SNU, NYU). New on arXiv today, 26 upvotes on HF.
  - **How it works:** it takes multi-view video history plus a language instruction and predicts future video from any camera, plus 3D trajectories of persistent scene points. A trajectory head reads the 3D tracks from the model's own attention features. Supervising the tracks ties appearance across cameras to one shared 3D motion. For robot control, action tokens are denoised together with the future frames.
  - **Data:** VPM-Data has 10K simulated clips (8,000 tabletop clips with a GR-1 humanoid, 2,000 urban clips with moving cameras). Each clip has synchronized views, intrinsics and extrinsics, persistent 3D tracks with per-view visibility, depth and captions. VP-WorldBench has 1,200 multi-view clips (sim/real × robot/general) and scores appearance, cross-view consistency and 3D motion.
  - **Claims:** better video fidelity and 3D motion accuracy than a video-only baseline, and higher manipulation success in sim and on a real robot. The abstract gives no figures. A 1M-sample dataset and a Cosmos3-Super version are promised for Dec 21.
  - **Why it matters for perception:** the dataset has exact simulator labels for 3D tracking, depth and multi-view consistency, which makes it useful for training or testing trackers even if you never touch world models.
- **Learning to Discriminate Scene Structures Makes Self-Supervised Depth Learning Scalable (MaSS)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23600), [code](https://github.com/mengtanZ/MaSS), [weights](https://huggingface.co/mengtanZ/MaSS/). From Zhang, Cui, Ma, Guo, Ye and Fan (Tongji University), NeurIPS 2026. It was posted Oct 16 and surfaced on HF Daily Papers this week; it has not been covered here before.
  - **The idea:** self-supervised depth usually gets *worse* when you mix indoor and outdoor data. MaSS separates a contextual pathway (semantics) from a structural pathway (spatial layout) and trains the structural one with a scene-structure discrimination objective. Trainable geometric features interact both ways with a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14.
  - **Numbers (Abs Rel / δ₁, median scaling):** mixed training beats per-dataset training on KITTI (0.092 / 0.913 vs 0.094 / 0.909), DDAD (0.125 / 0.862), NYUv2 (0.104 / 0.890) and nuScenes (0.134 / 0.852). Zero-shot, mixed training improves all eight sets, for example Cityscapes 0.132 → 0.103, DrivingStereo 0.131 → 0.100 and 7-Scenes 0.144 → 0.120.
  - **Running it:** the reference stack is old (Python 3.9, PyTorch 2.0.1, CUDA 11.7, xFormers 0.0.20).
- **KoalaGS: Complexity-Aware Continuous Level of Detail for Gaussian Splatting** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28615), [project](https://rdbch.github.io/koalags/). From Beche (TU Cluj-Napoca) and de Charette (Inria), a NeurIPS 2026 Spotlight. One set of Gaussians has to render well at any prefix budget. An image-complexity prior computed from the input views biases densification toward complex regions and scores which Gaussians to keep, and a variance loss keeps error even across budgets. On MipNeRF360 with 3.09M Gaussians, it gets 27.82 PSNR at 10% (MGS 26.97) and 16.04 at 0.1% (MGS 14.49), at near-equal full-capacity quality (29.77). Over a simulated 50 Mbps link it reaches 25 dB in 1.5 s, against 2.0 s for MGS. **Caveat:** at small budgets it renders more slowly than MGS on an H100 (indoor 1%: 475 vs 731 FPS). Code is "coming soon".
- **Think Visually Across Space and Time: Grounded Reasoning for Video Segmentation (TCoVP)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25090), [project](https://victor-wjw.github.io/TCoVP/). From Wen, Miao, Lu, Ming-Hsuan Yang, Li and Pei. For reasoning video segmentation, it replaces text-only chain-of-thought with steps that each bind an entity to a time range and timestamped spatial evidence. The chosen steps then become box prompts and temporal support for the mask predictor. It is trained with SFT on 15k examples, then chain-aligned RL on 13k. The authors report strong results on five VRS benchmarks with three backbones, but the page shows no scores. Code and models are promised.
- **Vision as Proprioception: Estimating Humanoid Robot Pose from Video (HumanoidPose)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25288). It combines pretrained human-pose features with robot kinematics to predict world-space joint configurations of humanoids from video. Synthetic retargeting across 16 embodiments plus self-training on online videos cuts joint-angle error by 18–32% on three benchmarks. We could read only a truncated summary, which did not name the benchmarks.

## Models & releases

- **supervision 0.31.1** — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/supervision/0.31.1/), [diff](https://github.com/roboflow/supervision/compare/0.31.0...0.31.1). Uploaded Oct 20, four days after 0.31.0. It is a bug-fix release with several metric fixes:
  - **Metrics:** `MeanAverageRecall` skips COCO crowd and ignored targets, matches predictions in confidence order, and fixes threshold matching for some oriented boxes. float16 box areas, mAP and `with_nmm` no longer overflow. Missing COCO segmentation areas are now computed from the mask.
  - **Annotators and I/O:** `MaskAnnotator` and `HaloAnnotator` accept grayscale images, and vertex annotators accept 3-coordinate keypoints. The no-OpenCV paths now draw closer to OpenCV and handle video files whose audio stream comes first.
- **Latent Bridge (HUST + Horizon Robotics)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28521), [code](https://github.com/hustvl/Latent-Bridge), [project](https://shuaizhang7.github.io/latent-bridge-page/). New today, from Xinggang Wang's group. A small trained "Bridge" maps WAN or V-RAE video-generation latents onto frozen VGGT-Omega features. This decodes 49-frame sequences straight to depth, cameras and point clouds without decoding to RGB first. Training and data-prep code are out, but the weights are "coming soon" and VGGT-Omega needs separate authorized access. The README gives no accuracy numbers.

## Today's deep read

**MaSS** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23600), [code](https://github.com/mengtanZ/MaSS), [weights](https://huggingface.co/mengtanZ/MaSS/).

- **What to read:**
  - **The failure it fixes:** why photometric self-supervision degrades under mixed indoor and outdoor data. Look at the per-dataset vs mixed tables, where mixed wins everywhere for MaSS. The question is what baselines do in the same setting.
  - **The two pathways:** how the contextual and structural projectors are split, and what the scene-structure discrimination loss uses as positives and negatives. This is the core idea, and it may carry over to other self-supervised dense tasks.
  - **Frozen-backbone adaptation:** how trainable geometric features get into a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 through bidirectional interaction. Compare its cost with full fine-tuning or a DPT head.
  - **Zero-shot spread:** DIODE is still weak (0.289 Abs Rel), so check where it fails.
- **How it could apply to you:** if you have plenty of unlabeled video from several deployment sites (warehouse cameras, vehicles, indoor robots), this is a recipe for one depth model trained on all of them without LiDAR labels.
  - **Baseline first:** fine-tune the released mixed checkpoint on your own footage before training from scratch.
  - **Scale:** median scaling is used in every number, so you still need metric scale from somewhere (IMU, known camera height, or a metric teacher).
  - **Porting:** the pinned PyTorch 2.0.1 / CUDA 11.7 stack will need porting before you can export it with current TensorRT or ONNX Runtime.
