## TL;DR
- **NVIDIA CompileIQ now plugs into PyTorch Helion.** It searches ptxas compiler controls (register allocation, scheduling) the way an autotuner searches tile sizes. On an NVFP4 W4A4 decode GEMV on GB200 it gave 1.048x in repeat validation, and 1.069x with a multi-objective search. The authors expect 2–7% on kernels that are already tuned and double digits on less-tuned ones.
- **cuTile Mamba-2 forward kernels run 1.14–1.82x faster than the Triton reference** (mamba_ssm 2.3.2.post1) on one B300. The gap grows with sequence length and state size. This is relevant if you train SSM/Mamba vision backbones on Blackwell.
- **ARROW (RWTH Aachen) has open weights for feed-forward 4D reconstruction plus 3D point tracking** from any image set: moving-camera video, several streams, or unordered photos. It claims SOTA 3D tracking on WorldTrack and TAPVid-3D. The model has ~1B parameters, is fine-tuned from Depth Anything 3 Giant, and the weights are non-commercial.

## Top papers

- **ARROW: Arbitrary Reconstruction and Tracking of 4D Observations in the Wild** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01314), [code](https://github.com/ifradlin/arrow), [weights](https://huggingface.co/ifradlin/arrow). From Fradlin, Schmidt, Piekenbrinck, Knaebel, Martin Garcia and Leibe (RWTH Aachen). It was posted Oct 1, and inference code, weights and a viewer followed Oct 12. It had not been covered here before; it surfaced on HF Daily Papers today.
  - **How it works:** it extends D4RT-style point-query decoding with an order-invariant query mechanism. A query can target any input frame, whatever its viewpoint or capture time, so one model handles reconstruction, 3D point tracking and multi-view tracking.
  - **Claims:** SOTA 3D tracking on WorldTrack and TAPVid-3D. It beats dedicated multi-view trackers on an adapted RGB-only MVTracker benchmark and stays competitive on reconstruction. Neither the abstract nor the model card gives the scores.
  - **Running it:** ~1B parameters, fine-tuned from DA3-GIANT-1.1. It installs with `uv sync` (PyTorch for CUDA 13.0, or MPS on Apple Silicon). The weights are CC BY-NC 4.0 and also subject to Waymo terms. Training and evaluation code are "coming next".
- **V-JEPA Hand: 3D hand and camera trajectories from egocentric video** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26780), [code](https://github.com/qft-333/V-JEPA-Hand), [weights](https://huggingface.co/qft-333/V-JEPA-Hand), [demo](https://huggingface.co/spaces/qft-333/V-JEPA-Hand). New on arXiv today. A fine-tuned V-JEPA 2.1 encoder (0.6B parameters, 2.43 GB checkpoint) reconstructs both hands as MANO meshes and recovers the camera trajectory in one forward pass. It needs no hand boxes and no calibration; focal length is predicted when intrinsics are not given. Inference runs in non-overlapping 16-frame windows with no smoothing across windows, in BF16 on CUDA by default. We could not read the abstract (arXiv 429), and the card gives no accuracy or speed figures. It is a useful sign that V-JEPA 2.1 features fine-tune well for dense 3D regression.
- **VideoMaMa++: Temporally Consistent Video Matting via Preserve-and-Refine Tokens** — [link](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/148674), [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=vxJjCRu1Km). From Lim, Oh, Yoon, Kim and Lee; it is a NeurIPS 2026 poster and appeared on arXiv today. Diffusion matting processes 6–24 frames per chunk, which makes edges flicker at chunk boundaries. Learnable "preserve" and "refine" tokens let the model carry previous mattes across chunks in a sliding window, and a shared reference anchor at every chunk limits drift on long videos without extra training. The page gives no numbers, and code and weights are promised but not yet released.

## Edge & deployment

- **PDNet: pruning plus distillation for real-time activity recognition** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26491), [VISAPP 2026 PDF](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2026/144772/144772.pdf). The recipe is L1 structured channel pruning of X3D-M (up to 25%, using Torch-Pruning), then distillation from a SlowFast teacher (T=4, α=0.7). On MECCANO (61 assembly actions), Top-1 goes from 36.49% to 39.46%, model size from 21.7 MB to 6.9 MB, compute from 6.2 to 3.78 GFLOPs, and CPU latency from 107.7 to 80.8 ms per 16-frame clip on an EPYC 7502P. Pruning alone drops Top-1 to 25.5%, so the distillation does the recovery. Mean per-class accuracy fell slightly (12.0% to 10.95%), which shows the long tail getting worse.

## GPU / CUDA / PyTorch

- **CompileIQ + Helion: tuning ptxas controls** — [blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/one-search-two-layers-compiler-controls-inside-pytorch-helion-using-nvidia-compileiq/), [CompileIQ](https://github.com/NVIDIA/CompileIQ), [Helion](https://github.com/pytorch/helion). Posted Oct 19, from NVIDIA and Meta.
  - **What it does:** an evolutionary search over ptxas controls, producing a portable Advanced Control File (ACF) that Helion applies at compile time. Pin a known-good ACF in `helion.Config(advanced_controls_file=...)`, or pass candidates to `autotune_search_acf` so they are searched jointly with block sizes and warps. The no-ACF default is always kept as a candidate.
  - **Numbers:** NVFP4 GEMV (N=8192, K=28672) on GB200 with CUDA 13.3: 0.0975 to 0.0930 ms (1.048x) across two validation runs. A runtime-plus-L1TEX objective reached 1.069x. One ACF tuned across two shapes gave 1.05x and 1.01x.
  - **Caveats:** ACFs are specific to the GPU architecture and toolkit (an sm_90 ACF is invalid on sm_100), so a correctness check is required. CompileIQ itself is under a proprietary NVIDIA EULA, but the ACFs it produces can be redistributed. A B200 "booster pack" of 24 ACFs is published.
- **Mamba-2 forward pass in CUDA Tile (cuTile)** — [blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerating-mamba-2-with-high-performance-forward-pass-kernels-in-nvidia-cuda-tile/), [TileGym](https://github.com/NVIDIA/TileGym). Posted Oct 19. Jane Street wrote three kernels in NVIDIA's Python tile DSL with NVIDIA's help: chunk cumsum, chunk-boundary states using Tensor Cores, and a fused output kernel. Optimizations include Blackwell Tensor Memory operand swaps, TMA-friendly layouts and per-shape autotuning. They run 1.14–1.82x faster than the Triton reference on one B300 (CUDA 13.3, cuda-tile 1.5.0, PyTorch 2.13). These are forward-only, kernel-level results; there is no backward pass yet and no end-to-end number.
- **TorchNeuron, a native PyTorch backend for AWS Trainium** — [blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/introducing-torchneuron-native-pytorch-backend-for-aws-trainium/), [repo](https://github.com/aws-neuron/torch-neuronx). Public beta from Oct 20, Apache-2.0. Models move with `.to('neuron')`, there are eager and `torch.compile(backend="neuron")` paths, FSDP2, tensor parallelism and DTensor are supported, and custom kernels go through NKI. The only performance figure is a customer claim (Deepgram, ~60% more audio seconds per dollar), with nothing for vision.

## Today's deep read

**CompileIQ + Helion** — [blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/one-search-two-layers-compiler-controls-inside-pytorch-helion-using-nvidia-compileiq/), [Helion ACF examples](https://github.com/pytorch/helion).

- **What to read:**
  - **The joint-search section:** why tuning block size and ptxas register policy one after the other gets stuck in local optima, since tile size and register pressure are coupled. Also check what joint search costs in extra compile-and-measure trials.
  - **The objectives:** runtime alone (1.048x), occupancy (~2%), and runtime plus L1TEX throughput (1.069x, L1TEX from 81.5% to 86.0%). That progression shows which hardware counters to target for a memory-bound kernel.
  - **The validation method:** two independent runs, p-values and Cohen's d. This is a template for showing that a 5% kernel gain is real and not noise.
  - **The caveats:** ACFs are tied to the architecture and the ptxas version, and must be re-validated after every CUDA upgrade.
- **How it could apply to you:** most CV inference stacks now have a few custom Triton or Helion kernels: fused pre- and post-processing, NMS-free decoding heads, deformable attention, INT8/FP8 GEMMs for ViT backbones. Those are the "less-optimized" kernels where the authors expect double-digit gains.
  - **Offline search:** run CompileIQ once per (kernel, shape, GPU, CUDA version), pin the winning ACF in the kernel config, and gate it behind a correctness test in CI.
  - **Where it fits:** it is a cheap layer below the autotuning you already do. Because the default config always stays a candidate, the search cannot pick something slower than what you have today.
  - **Licensing:** check the EULA before adding CompileIQ to a shipped toolchain. The ACF files it outputs can be redistributed.
