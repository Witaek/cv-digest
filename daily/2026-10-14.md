## Top papers

- **Growing to See: Efficient Pretraining of Self-Supervised Vision Transformers** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16817). Per its title, it cuts the cost of self-supervised ViT pretraining (DINO/MAE-style), likely by starting small and growing the network during training. If the savings hold at ViT-B/L scale, in-domain pretraining gets cheaper. That is the usual alternative to fine-tuning a generic DINO or SigLIP encoder on industrial, medical or aerial imagery. Check the abstract for compute savings and linear-probe/dense-task results against a fixed-size baseline.
- **Segment Where You Look: Attention as the Interface Between Language Models and Segmenters** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18729). Instead of training a VLM to emit a mask token (the LISA approach), it reads where the language model attends in the image and passes that to a segmenter as the prompt. That could make referring segmentation work with off-the-shelf VLMs and SAM-class segmenters without joint fine-tuning. Check which segmenter it uses and how it compares to mask-token methods on RefCOCO-style benchmarks.
- **One Block, Multiple Depths: Recurrent Vision Transformers with Depth-Programmed Experts** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12448). A ViT that reuses one block recurrently, with experts that specialize by iteration. This trades parameters for compute and could allow choosing depth at inference time. That is the interesting part for edge use: one set of weights could serve several latency budgets.
- **Squeeze-Aware-YOLO: Early Channel Recalibration and Multi-Scale Fusion for Real-Time Traffic Sign Detection** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15604). A YOLO variant that adds squeeze-and-excitation-style channel attention early in the network, plus multi-scale fusion for small objects. It is incremental, but traffic signs are a standard small-object test case. Check whether the latency cost of the extra attention is reported on real hardware.

## Today's deep read

**Growing to See: Efficient Pretraining of Self-Supervised Vision Transformers** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16817).

- **What to read:** how the model grows (depth, width or patch count, and when), how weights are initialized when it grows, and the compute saved compared with training the final-size model from the start. Then look at downstream results that matter for perception: dense tasks (segmentation, depth, detection with a frozen or fine-tuned backbone), not only ImageNet linear probing.
- **How it could apply to you:** many production teams skip in-domain self-supervised pretraining because a ViT-B run costs too many GPU-days. If a growth schedule gets close to full-size quality at a fraction of the compute, pretraining on your own unlabeled images becomes practical. Then compare that backbone with a fine-tuned public DINO-class encoder on your task. The things to verify are whether the paper's recipe is compatible with your SSL objective, and whether the savings still hold at the image resolution you train on.
