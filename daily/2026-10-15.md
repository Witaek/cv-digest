## Top papers

- **TAG: Calibrating SAM3 for Universal Image Segmentation without Dense Supervision** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14651). Per its title, it turns SAM 3 (concept-prompted segmentation) into a universal segmenter, apparently by calibrating its outputs rather than retraining with dense mask labels. If it works, it is a cheap route to class-labeled semantic/panoptic maps from a frozen foundation segmenter. Check which benchmarks (COCO panoptic, ADE20K) and what weak supervision it uses.
- **SAM3RL: Memory Control via Reinforcement Learning for Visual Object Tracking** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19931). SAM 2/3 track objects through a memory bank of past frames, filled by fixed rules. This paper learns which frames to keep with reinforcement learning. Memory selection is where SAM-style trackers fail on occlusion and distractors, so check results on long-video benchmarks (LaSOT, LVOS-style) and whether the policy adds latency.
- **Self-Supervised Learning of Semantically Consistent Visual Features via a Simple 3D Prior** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16988). Adds a 3D prior to self-supervised pretraining so features stay consistent for the same object part across views. Multi-view consistency is a known weakness of DINO-class features for correspondence and 3D tasks. Look for dense-task results (keypoint correspondence, segmentation) against DINOv2/v3.
- **Fast Pose Tracking of Rigid Objects with Compact Pose Graph Optimization** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11815). 6-DoF tracking of rigid objects using a small pose graph, presumably to keep optimization cost bounded over long sequences. Relevant to robotics and AR pipelines where a pose estimator runs per frame and drift has to be corrected cheaply. Check the reported FPS and hardware.

## Edge & deployment

- **Seeing at the Edge: Energy Characterization of Vision-Language Model Inference on Edge Platforms** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20136). A measurement study of VLM inference energy on edge hardware. Measured energy (not just latency) is rare in papers and is what decides battery and thermal budgets. Check which devices, models and precisions it covers, and whether the vision encoder or the decoder dominates.
- **VisionMX: Unlocking Microscaling Post-Training Quantization for Vision Models** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03218). *(Early October, not covered before.)* Post-training quantization of vision models to microscaling (MX) formats such as MXFP8/MXFP4, which recent NVIDIA GPUs support in hardware. Worth reading if you plan FP4/FP8 deployment of ViT backbones and want to know where PTQ breaks.

## Today's deep read

**TAG: Calibrating SAM3 for Universal Image Segmentation without Dense Supervision** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14651).

- **What to read:** first, what "calibrating" means here: score/threshold calibration of SAM 3's per-concept masks, a small trained head, or something else. Then what supervision it replaces dense masks with (image-level tags, boxes, pseudo-labels), and how it handles overlaps between concepts to produce one non-overlapping panoptic map. Finally, the gap to fully supervised specialists (Mask2Former-class) on the standard benchmarks, and the runtime cost of querying many concepts per image.
- **How it could apply to you:** many teams already run SAM 3 to auto-label data but still train a dedicated segmenter because SAM's raw outputs are not calibrated across classes. If TAG closes most of that gap with weak labels, you could either deploy the calibrated SAM 3 directly (when latency allows) or use it to produce much cleaner pseudo-labels for distilling a small real-time segmenter. Verify whether the method needs per-dataset calibration, which would mean a recalibration step every time your class list changes.
