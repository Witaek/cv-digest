## Top papers

- **RiO-DETRv2: Geometry-Aware Uncertainty Modeling Meets Real-Time Oriented Object Detection Transformer** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29159), [code](https://github.com/RicePasteM/RiO-DETRv2). From Zhangchi Hu, …, Xiaoyan Sun.
  - **The idea:** three changes to the RiO-DETR decoder.
    - **Object-aligned boundary distributions:** each box edge is a probability distribution in the object's own frame, refined across decoder layers (the DFL/D-FINE idea, applied to rotated boxes).
    - **Circular-to-local angle refinement:** the angle goes from a coarse global periodic estimate to a local residual.
    - **Unified OBB quality-aligned learning:** classification, boundary and angle refinement are all supervised against full oriented-box quality.
  - **Numbers:** RiO-DETRv2-l/x reach 53.7/54.1 mAP50-95 on DOTA, +1.2/+1.5 over RiO-DETR-l/x, "with nearly unchanged compute". The listing abstract gives no FPS or GFLOPs; check the paper's tables.
  - **Why it matters:** an NMS-free, real-time oriented detector for aerial, document or warehouse imagery, a niche still dominated by YOLO-OBB variants.
- **OccJEPA: Occlusion as Masking for Amodal Video Segmentation** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29061), [code](https://github.com/zgp110/occjepa). Single author (Genpei Zhang).
  - **How it works:** tokens outside the visible mask are dropped, a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 predictor fills them in (the same job it did in pretraining), and a light head decodes the full amodal mask. Visible masks can come from SAM2 tracking.
  - **Numbers:** deterministic and 33.5x faster than the diffusion-based SOTA. Gains on SAIL-VOS, TAO-Amodal (+4.00 AP50 on a held-out split) and Kubric. The gains track the predictor's pretrained weights, tested with world models from 87M to 1.85B parameters.
  - **Why it matters:** a concrete use of JEPA-style predictors as a perception module, not just a pretraining trick.
- **Register Anything Model (RAM) for Generalizable and Robust Point Cloud Registration** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28821), [code + models](https://github.com/qinzheng93/RegisterAnything). From Zheng Qin, …, Kai Xu; accepted at NeurIPS 2026, posted Oct 20.
  - One model trained jointly on indoor, outdoor and object data. A geometry-aware normalization adaptively voxelizes and rescales point clouds to absorb scale, noise and density differences, and a coarse-to-fine geometric transformer models local rather than global consistency.
  - Claims SOTA inlier ratio and registration recall across 12 benchmarks, beating domain-specific models. The abstract gives no per-benchmark numbers.
- **NeuralSRender: G-Buffer Guided Video Diffusion for Sim-to-Real Rendering** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29200), [code](https://github.com/WHU-USI3DV/NeuralSRender). A pretrained video DiT is conditioned on simulator G-buffers via a small projector plus LoRA (1.45% parameter overhead, trainable on a 24 GB GPU). Training perception models on the translated driving videos lifts nuImages detection AP50 from 35.1 to 40.6 and Cityscapes mIoU from 32.4 to 40.8. Included because the payoff is measured on perception, not on FID.

## Edge & deployment

- **DetM-Tracker: Deterministic and Memory-Efficient VIS for Autonomous Industrial Inspection** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29212). Video instance segmentation for edge hardware. It condenses each instance's memory into four scalars (temporal memory goes from O(T·N·R) to O(T·N̂)), sizes queries to the number of visible objects, and uses an entropy gate to spend compute only on uncertain boundaries. It reports about 20x lower latency and 6x lower VRAM; the listing truncated the rest of the abstract, so the baseline and hardware are unknown. No code is linked.
- **A Unified Evaluation Framework of Physical Adversarial Robustness in the Edge VLM Era** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29196), [code](https://github.com/ericliang1/physical-adversarial-robustness). Compares wearable adversarial patches against compact grounding VLMs and classic detectors. It introduces a conditional attack success rate (counted only on images still localized under a matched gray occluder) and splits failures into omission, substitution and displacement. Edge VLMs are as vulnerable as detectors, and the failure mode depends more on output format than on architecture. Only omission is visible downstream, which matters if you swap a detector for a small VLM in a safety pipeline.
- **Cache Locally, Recompute Selectively for Streaming Video Understanding** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29229). On RVS-Ego it cuts per-frame prefill FLOPs by over 20% and total processing time by 14–59% for retrieval-based streaming video LLMs, with accuracy up to +3.9 pp.

## GPU / CUDA / PyTorch

- **Accelerating PyTorch on AMD GPUs with FlyDSL** — [blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/accelerating-pytorch-on-amd-gpus-with-flydsl/), [repo](https://github.com/ROCm/FlyDSL). Posted Oct 21. FlyDSL is AMD's MLIR-based kernel DSL with explicit layout algebra (closer to CuTe than to Triton).
  - **Usage:** `config.max_autotune_gemm_backends = "ATEN,TRITON,FLYDSL"` with `mode="max-autotune"`; eligible `F.rms_norm` and `torch.topk` dispatch automatically in eager mode; SDPA goes through AOTriton 0.14b. MI350-series (gfx950) only, `flydsl>=0.3.0,<0.4`.
  - **Kernel numbers (MI355X):** BF16 dense GEMM 1.10x geomean over the faster of ATen/Triton; MXFP8 / MXFP4 scaled GEMM 1.58x / 1.68x over ATen; grouped GEMM 1.20x over Triton; TopK at small K 4.82x.
  - **End-to-end (vLLM, one MI355X):** BF16 up to 1.13x; MXFP8 1.04–2.04x; MXFP4 1.25–3.01x (single runs per cell).
- **TorchTitan update: new models, performance at scale, TitanRL** — [blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/torchtitan-update-new-sota-models-performance-at-scale-titanrl/). Posted Oct 21. The vision-relevant part: Qwen3-VL is now supported natively, with shared vision components, multimodal data pipelines and HF checkpoint interop. Also: torch_checkpointing cuts trainer blocking from 841.2 ms to 34.1 ms (DSv3 671B, 512 H100s), and a new opt-in NCCL backend ships in PyTorch 2.14.
- **Reading data into GPU memory in Python with GPUDirect Storage** — [blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-to-read-data-into-gpu-memory-in-python-with-nvidia-gpudirect-storage/). Posted Oct 22. A practical KvikIO walkthrough (`pip install kvikio-cu13`, `kvikio.CuFile(path).read(gpu_buf)`), including how to check with `CUFILE_STATS_LEVEL=3` and `gds_stats` that reads really take the `nvfs` path rather than silently falling back to POSIX compatibility mode. There are no throughput numbers. Useful if you load large image or tensor shards straight to GPU.

## Today's deep read

**RiO-DETRv2** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29159), [code](https://github.com/RicePasteM/RiO-DETRv2).

- **What to read:**
  - **Speed table:** the abstract only says "nearly unchanged compute". Find the FPS, GPU, precision and whether TensorRT was used, and compare against YOLO26-OBB and YOLO11-OBB at the same resolution.
  - **Boundary distributions:** how the object-aligned edge distributions differ from D-FINE's fine-grained distribution refinement, and whether the decoder still runs in a fixed number of layers (important for export).
  - **Angle refinement:** how the circular-to-local scheme handles the square-box and 90° ambiguity cases that break naive angle regression.
  - **Ablation:** how much of the +1.5 comes from each of the three parts, so you can port only the cheap ones.
- **How it could apply to you:** if you run oriented detection (aerial, shelf, parcel, PCB) with YOLO-OBB and NMS tuning is a pain, this is an NMS-free alternative with code.
  - **Baseline first:** train it on your own data next to your current OBB model at the same input size and report mAP50-95 and end-to-end latency, including postprocessing.
  - **Export check:** before committing, confirm that the distribution heads and angle refinement export cleanly to ONNX/TensorRT; DETR decoders with custom ops are where deployments usually stall.
